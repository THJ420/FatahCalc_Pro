How the Data Storage works

Every storage unit on this page is stored as a single number: how many bytes it represents. Converting is then always the same two-step move, no matter which of the {n{'}'} directions you pick:

value_in_base = input × factor(from) result = value_in_base ÷ factor(to)

That is why FatahCalc Pro needs one storage page instead of one page per pair — the dropdowns simply choose which two factors get plugged into the same formula. Results are rounded to 10 significant digits and shown in scientific notation when they get extreme.

Reference factors

  • 1 byte = 8 bits
  • Decimal prefixes step by 1,000: 1 MB = 1,000,000 B
  • Binary prefixes step by 1,024: 1 MiB = 1,048,576 B
  • The gap grows with scale: 1 TB is only 0.909 TiB — about 9% "missing"

Why your 1 TB drive shows 931 GB

Drive manufacturers sell decimal terabytes (10¹² bytes) while Windows reports binary tebibytes but labels them "GB". 10¹² ÷ 1024³ = 931.3, so nothing is actually missing — the two systems are just counting in different bases. Both families are listed here so you can compare them directly.

Bits vs bytes

Network speeds are quoted in bits (100 Mbps), file sizes in bytes (12 MB). Divide by 8 to go from bits to bytes: a 100 Mbps line moves about 12.5 MB per second at best.

Data Storage FAQ

Why does a 1 TB drive show less space in my operating system?

Drive makers use decimal units (1 TB = 10¹² bytes), while many operating systems report binary units (1 TiB = 2⁴⁰ bytes). The converter shows both.

Is a kilobyte 1,000 or 1,024 bytes?

Decimal KB is 1,000 bytes; binary KiB is 1,024 bytes. Both are included.
